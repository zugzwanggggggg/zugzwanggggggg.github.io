OSCP Review

TLDR; My OSCP journey began in mid-August 2024. I was unable to compromise any Active Directory (AD) Machines on my first try. To prepare for my second attempt, I played with Hack the Box boxes. On my second attempt, I successfully passed the OSCP+.

As of 16 March 2025, I've successfully completed the OSCP.

VIEW CREDENTIAL

Kicking It Off

I was first offered an opportunity to take up this cert (fully sponsored) due to my consistent efforts in WorldSkills training. At that time, I was still a noob at hacking, with only experience from a ethical hacking module in school. Everyone was also talking about how important the cert was as a HR filter when applying to jobs. So ultimately, I took it up during my 2-month vacation.

Course Content

The course had a total of 27 modules ranging from the fundamentals like report writing, all the way to attacking AD environments. It also included modules that weren't tested, such as attacks on AWS Cloud Infrastructure.

Since I did have some experience in the basics (e.g. Nmap familiarity, simple web exploitations), I was able to complete some modules fairly quickly. On the other hand, the AD modules took me a day each as I was unfamiliar with the content and wanted to spend more time understanding the concepts.

The course bundle also included access to their challenge labs and (limited) proving grounds practice

The Exam

While waiting for the proctor to enter the session, I was very nervous as it was my first time. Once the time started, I began running my enumeration commands and screenshotting every output. Around 2 hours of enumerating, I couldn't get a single machine yet, so I took a quick break before continuing on.

By the next day, I managed to get 60 points, falling short on the passing score.

I took a couple weeks break practicing on Hack the Box boxes with none other than TJ Null's list of OSCP-like boxes.

With a refined methodology, my second attempt was more enjoyable. I played some classical music (as suggested by a blog) and suddenly realised that the AD set was an assume-breach. OH MY GOD. I spent 2 whole hours panicking because I missed this detail.

I quickly accessed the machine, pivoted and compromised the entire set in 2 hours. A few days later, I received an email with the result that I passed! (Yay)

Tips

  • Get familiar with the structure of the exam by practicing on practice machines
  • Stay calm
  • DO NOT end your exam without checking that you have all your screenshots
  • Play some classical music when you're stuck :)
